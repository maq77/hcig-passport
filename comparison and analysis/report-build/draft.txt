10 WHAT THE CEO WILL ASK

Ten questions, short answers.

1 Which two do we buy?

    D, the Allrounder, as the emergency ambulance. B, the HotCountry Sprinter, for transfers. Combined score
    8.2 and 8.0, the top two.

2 What will they cost us in total?

    About 230k to 295k ex works. About 273k to 350k landed in Egypt, before freight and licensing. Firm
    numbers need a WAS quote.

3 Why not two of the cheapest?

    B has a manual ramp stretcher and a smaller layout. A critical tourist patient needs a full emergency
    ambulance. One D covers that. B covers the high volume of simple transfers.

4 Why not the MAN with the best stretcher?

    It is the most expensive by far, built to Dutch rules, and MAN's van service in Egypt is not proven. A
    breakdown far from Cairo could stop it for days.

5 Do we need 4x4?

    Only for desert, safari or beach calls. If yes, order B with the 4x4 option first. A is heavier and costs more for
    less clinical kit.

6 Can we service them in Hurghada?

    Yes for the Sprinter models. Mercedes-Benz Egypt has an authorised workshop in Hurghada. WAS has a
    sales partner in Cairo. Confirm the Hurghada site takes Sprinter vans.

7 Will a European engine survive Egyptian diesel?

    Not as a standard Euro 6 engine. The quote must state an engine and emission level made for Egyptian fuel.
    WAS has built 1,000 for Egypt and knows this market.

8 How long until delivery?

    Not published. It depends on Mercedes chassis supply. WAS reported chassis delays in 2021. Ask for a
    delivery date and a late delivery penalty in the contract.

9 What licence do we need to run them?

    A technical conformity certificate from the Egyptian Ambulance Organisation, renewed yearly, and trained
    crews. Check the current fee and rules before delivery.

10 What are they worth later?

    Sprinter ambulances keep a resale market. Used WAS Sprinter ambulances from 2012 and 2013 still list at
    10,900 to 13,800.32
