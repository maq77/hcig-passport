A good ambulance in Germany can be a poor one in Hurghada. These five points weigh as much as the specification.

09Each vehicle in detail · A and B

AKTW Type B Civil Protection 4x4

Sprinter 519 CDI AWD · 190 PS · automatic · 5,380 kg · score 6.2 · est. €115k to €140k

Designed for German disaster units: patient transport after major incidents, where normal ambulances cannot pass. Germany's federal civil protection agency bought 180 of this type.10,11

Strengths

  • Only 4x4 of the four, with rough road chassis and aluminium underbody guard.
  • Two stretcher positions.
  • Electro hydraulic stretcher table with height adjustment and side shift.
  • Strong 9 kW air conditioning.

Weaknesses

  • Transport ambulance. Medical kit only prepared, not fitted.
  • Heaviest at 5.38 t: more fuel, tyre and brake wear.
  • Civil protection details HCIG does not need.
  • Owner reports of 4x4 electronic faults.26,27
Verdict. Buy only if desert track rescue with two patients is a real, regular need.

BALS/BLS Hot‑Country

Sprinter 315 CDI · 150 PS · 3,500 kg (now 3,880 kg) · score 8.0 · est. €80k to €105k

WAS's export ambulance for hard climates. The interior is "especially designed for very hot countries".2 The datasheet lists a full emergency kit.

Strengths

  • Lowest price and best value index (8.0).
  • Heat proof materials, constant airflow, heavy duty AC, solar fan.
  • Defibrillator, monitor, suction, nebuliser, vacuum splints listed.
  • Light, so easy in towns and resort roads. 4x4 now listed.5

Weaknesses

  • Manual Ferno roll in stretcher on a ramp. Crews rate it unstable.25
  • Datasheet from 2020. Some items may have changed.
  • At 3.5 t the payload is tight. Order the 3.88 t version.
  • Smallest engine of the four.
Verdict. Pick 2. Order at 3.88 t and ask WAS to price a better stretcher system.

Healthcare International GroupQuestions and answers
10What the CEO will ask

Ten questions, short answers.

1Which two do we buy?
D, the Allrounder, as the emergency ambulance. B, the Hot‑Country Sprinter, for transfers. Scores 8.2 and 8.0, the top two. Also ask WAS to quote two D units: once B gets a stretcher table and 3.88 t, the price gap narrows.
2What will they cost us in total?
About €230k to €295k ex works. About €273k to €350k landed in Egypt, before freight and licensing. Firm numbers need a WAS quote.
3Why not two of the cheapest?
B has a manual ramp stretcher and a smaller layout. A critical tourist patient needs a full emergency ambulance. One D covers that. B covers the high volume of simple transfers.
4Why not the MAN with the best stretcher?
It is the most expensive by far, built to Dutch rules, and MAN's van service in Egypt is not proven. A breakdown far from Cairo could stop it for days.
5Do we need 4x4?
Only for desert tracks and safari calls. If yes, price B with 4x4 first. A is heavier and costs more for less clinical fit out. No van of this size works on soft beach sand.
6Can we service them in Hurghada?
The base van, yes: Mercedes-Benz Egypt has an authorised workshop in Hurghada. The ambulance conversion (control panel, roof AC, stretcher table) is WAS work. Require wiring diagrams, diagnostic access and a spare parts kit with delivery.
7Will a European engine survive Egyptian diesel?
Not as a standard Euro 6 engine. The quote must state an engine and emission level made for Egyptian fuel. On a two vehicle order WAS may use stock European chassis, so ask how the 2026 Egypt batch was built, or source an Egypt spec chassis locally.
8How long until delivery?
Not published. It depends on Mercedes chassis supply. WAS reported chassis delays in 2021. Ask for a delivery date and a late delivery penalty in the contract.
9What licence do we need to run them?
A technical conformity certificate from the Egyptian Ambulance Organisation, renewed yearly, and trained crews. Check the current fee and rules before delivery.
10What are they worth later?
Sprinter ambulances keep a resale market. Used WAS Sprinter ambulances from 2012 and 2013 still list at €10,900 to €13,800.32
