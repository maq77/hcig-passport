01

Every encounter dies at the airport

What actually happens today, and where the money leaks out

The clinical operation is excellent. The memory is non-existent.

A German guest at a Hurghada resort sprains an ankle. The hotel calls. A 24/7 Clinic doctor is in her room in twenty minutes. Good care, insurance billed, case closed. Eleven months later she is back on the same coast — and to us she is a stranger. We re-take her history through a language barrier. We re-do the X-ray because we cannot see the old one. If she instead walks into MedPark Sahl Hasheesh, she is a stranger there too, because our hospitals and our resort clinics do not share a patient identity.

The reframe for this meeting

This is not "let's build an app." It is: HCIG has no Master Patient Index. A group running around 150 care points cannot say how many unique patients it has ever treated. The app is simply the most visible — and most monetisable — client of the identity layer we need regardless.

02

What we actually build

Three components, in dependency order

Component 1 · foundation

The identity spine

One HCIG ID per human being, created at first contact anywhere in the group — resort clinic, urgent care, hospital emergency. Passport or national ID scanned once, matched forever after.

Component 2 · the asset

The longitudinal record

Every encounter, allergy, prescription, lab and image attached to that ID in a standards-based store, readable by any HCIG clinician with consent — and exportable to the patient's own doctor at home.

Component 3 · the surface

The apps

What the guest, the clinician and the dispatcher each see. Routing to the nearest capable HCIG facility, live case tracking, cashless status, and the commercial layer on top.

The routing question, precisely

"Nearest HCIG facility" is not a straight distance sort. A guest in Marsa Alam with chest pain should not be routed to the closest dental chair. The dispatcher — human or automatic — scores every facility on five axes:

AxisWhat it readsWhy it changes the answer
ProximityGuest GPS or hotel geocode → facility coordinates, road time not straight lineAlong the Red Sea coast, road distance and straight-line distance diverge badly
CapabilityCapability tags per facility: CT, ICU, ventilator, dental chair, hyperbaric, obstetrics, imagingOnly MedPark sites carry CT and ICU; a diving accident has exactly one correct destination
LanguageOn-shift staff language coverage: DE, RU, CS, IT, FR, EN, ARA Czech guest sent to a shift with no Czech or German cover is a clinical risk, not an inconvenience
CoverageWhich insurers that facility bills cashless; the guest's active policyRouting to a site that cannot bill her insurer turns a cashless case into a payment dispute
LoadLive queue depth and doctor availability at that facilityNearest is worthless if the wait is ninety minutes and the next site is eight minutes further
Return detection

Three independent triggers tell us a known guest is back in Egypt: a hotel check-in event matching their ID, a geofence entry at Hurghada, Marsa Alam, Sharm or Cairo airports, or simply an app open inside Egypt. Any one of them fires the welcome-back flow, pre-loads their record and pins their nearest HCIG facility. This is the single feature the whole proposal turns on.

05

The next-gen layer

Six capabilities that are genuinely differentiating — each with the reason it pays for itself

Identity

Biometric re-identification

Passport MRZ scan plus optional on-device face match. A returning guest who lost their card, changed phone, or whose name is transliterated differently is still matched in under two seconds. Pays for itself: it is the mechanism that turns a one-off encounter into a lifetime relationship.

Language

Real-time medical translation

Speech-to-speech DE / RU / CS / IT / FR / PL ↔ EN / AR during the consultation, with a medical glossary and a transcript attached to the encounter. Pays for itself: removes the biggest clinical risk in our patient mix and lets us staff on clinical skill rather than language.

Documentation

Ambient clinical scribe

The consultation is transcribed and structured into a draft note the doctor reviews and signs. Never filed unreviewed. Pays for itself: complete, coded documentation is precisely what insurers reject claims for lacking.

Decision support

AI triage & protocol prompts

Symptom intake scores urgency and routes to the right capability tier. Clinician-side it surfaces protocol reminders — drug interactions against the record, dive-medicine red flags, sepsis criteria. Support, never diagnosis; a human always decides. Pays for itself: right-tier routing avoids both under-triage risk and expensive over-dispatch.

Operations

Demand forecasting

Resort occupancy, arrivals, season and historical incidence predict encounter volume per cluster, seven days ahead. Pays for itself: staffing ~150 sites on intuition is expensive in both directions — idle shifts and missed calls.

Resilience

Offline-first clinical client

Resort connectivity is unreliable and that will not change. The clinician app holds an encrypted local store, works fully offline and reconciles on reconnect with conflict resolution. Pays for itself: it is the difference between a system doctors use and one they abandon in week three.

Deliberately out of scope

Naming what we are not doing is how this proposal survives contact with a sceptical room. No autonomous AI diagnosis. No wearable-driven clinical alerting in v1 — read-only vitals import only. No blockchain health records. No replacement of MedPark's hospital information system. No health-condition-based advertising targeting, ever, for the reasons in section 09.

06

Preserving the medical history

How the record is built, matched, protected and handed on

The clinical value is simple: a doctor who can see the last four encounters makes a faster, safer, cheaper decision than one starting from a blank page across a language barrier. The commercial value is that this record is the only thing a competitor clinic cannot replicate.

Matching a human being across every HCIG facility

TierSignalConfidenceBehaviour
DeterministicPassport or national ID number + date of birthCertainAuto-merge into existing HCIG ID
Strong probabilisticName (transliteration-normalised) + DOB + nationality + phone or emailHighAuto-merge above threshold, logged for audit
Weak probabilisticPartial name + DOB + resort + arrival windowReviewQueued for human review — never auto-merged
BiometricFace template match, on device, opt-inHighConfirms a probabilistic match; never the sole basis
Self-assertGuest signs into their own accountCertainDirect link

Transliteration is a real problem here, not a theoretical one. Cyrillic and Arabic names arrive spelled four different ways across four visits. The normaliser and the review queue are not optional.

What we store, in a standard

Build on HL7 FHIR R4 rather than an in-house schema. It costs nothing extra at design time, and it means the record is portable, auditable, and already speaks the language of MedPark's hospital systems and any system we later integrate.

Patient · identity, demographics, nationality, languages
Encounter · each visit, facility, clinician
Condition · diagnoses, ICD-10 coded
AllergyIntolerance · the banner that prevents harm
MedicationRequest / MedicationStatement · prescribed and home meds
Observation · vitals, point-of-care results
DiagnosticReport + ImagingStudy · labs and DICOM references
Coverage + Claim · policy, GOP, billing
Consent · scope, purpose, expiry, withdrawal — first-class, not a checkbox
Organization / Location / Practitioner · the facility directory that drives routing

Handing it on: the International Patient Summary

On discharge the guest receives an IPS — the international standard summary of problems, allergies, medications, procedures and results. Delivered as a PDF in their own language plus a machine-readable FHIR bundle behind a QR code. Their doctor in Munich or Prague can read it without ever having heard of us.

This is the feature that makes the app worth keeping after the flight home, and the reason a guest opens it again next year rather than deleting it at baggage claim.

Raise this before the IT head does

Most of our patients are EU data subjects. GDPR applies to us extraterritorially because we offer services to them, and health data is Article 9 special-category. Egypt has no EU adequacy decision, so a Cairo-only datastore is not a defensible design. Section 11 sets out the answer; the short version is an EU-region primary store, Standard Contractual Clauses plus a transfer impact assessment for operational access from Egypt, and Egypt's PDPL 151/2018 handled in parallel. Budget a DPIA and external counsel from day one — roughly €8–15k, and it is the cheapest risk we will ever retire.

Why the recovered half is the strong half

New revenue asks a room to believe a forecast. Recovered revenue asks them to believe a measurement they can take next month. Before the pilot, pull three numbers from Finance: the current claim rejection and short-payment rate, the number of encounters where a diagnostic was repeated within twelve months, and the number of resort calls that ended without an HCIG encounter. Every one of those is a leak with a euro sign on it, and every one is closed by the identity spine alone — before a single guest downloads anything.

Fix the website before buying a single ad

We currently run three separate domains — healthcareig.com, 247clinic.net and medparkhospitals.com — competing for the same search terms and splitting our authority three ways. Consolidate into a hub-and-spoke: healthcareig.com as the authority hub with proper country subfolders (/de/ /ru/ /cs/ /it/), brand sites as spokes with canonical links back. Add hreflang, local business schema for every facility, and a real German-language content set answering "what do I do if I get ill in Hurghada". That work costs almost nothing and raises the return on every ad euro that follows it.

Advertising inside the app — and the hard limits

Permitted inventory

Direct-sold partner placements

Featured slots in the wellness and dental marketplace. Resort spa, dive centre and excursion partners. Diagnostic labs. OTC pharmacy brands. Insurer brand presence on the coverage screen. Priced as flat monthly placements, not programmatic — our audience is seasonal and high-value, which is worth more sold by hand.

Prohibited — non-negotiable

No ad networks, no health targeting

No AdMob or programmatic SDK: passing health-app signals to an ad network is a GDPR breach waiting to be found, and the eCPM would be trivial anyway. No targeting on health conditions — Article 9 makes that effectively unlawful. No prescription-drug advertising to consumers, which is illegal across the EU. And no advertising of any kind on the triage, active-case or medical-record screens.

What to measure

  • Install → register → first encounter → repeat encounter. Four numbers. Everything else is decoration.
  • Unique QR per property so every resort's contribution is attributable and factual.
  • Re-identification rate — of guests treated this season, how many were matched to a prior record. This is the single KPI that proves the thesis.
  • Lifetime value per registered guest across all facilities, a number HCIG has never been able to calculate.
  • Denial rate, app-documented encounters vs paper — run these side by side during the pilot so the finance case writes itself.

11

The questions IT will ask

Prepared answers · take these into the meeting rather than discovering them in it

MedPark already has hospital systems. Why another one?
We are not replacing anything. MedPark keeps its HIS; we read from it over HL7. The gap is that around 150 on-site resort clinics have no system at all, and no two facilities share a patient identity. This is an index and an integration layer above what exists, not a competitor to it.
GDPR. Most of these patients are EU citizens.
Correct, and it is the biggest single risk. The design answer: EU-region primary datastore, Standard Contractual Clauses plus a transfer impact assessment for operational access from Egypt (there is no adequacy decision), Article 9(2)(h) as the lawful basis for treatment records with separate explicit consent for the app and any marketing, a DPIA before the pilot because this is large-scale special-category processing, and multilingual consent capture with a working withdrawal path. Egypt's PDPL 151/2018 runs in parallel.
Who maintains this after you build it?
A fair question and the one that sinks most internal apps. The honest answer is that phases 0–1 are buildable by a small team, but steady state needs a named owner for the MPI review queue, clinical content and six-language support. That should be part of the approval, not an afterthought — I would rather argue it now than discover it in month nine.
Doctors will not use it. They never do.
They will not, if it costs them time. So phase 2 leads with the ambient scribe and offline capture — the two features that make a shift faster, not slower. The rule for the clinician client is that it must save more minutes than it takes, measured, or we stop and redesign. Adoption is a product problem, not a training problem.
Will the resorts cooperate? They guard their guest data.
They are not processors for us — they are separate controllers, and the handoff needs its own agreement. What makes them say yes is that this is a guest-safety differentiator they can market, and it cuts front-desk workload during a medical incident. Start with the ten properties where our relationship is strongest and let the results recruit the rest.
What if a guest asks us to delete their record?
They can, and we must honour it — with the standard medical-retention carve-out, which we document upfront rather than argue about later. Erasure removes the account, the identity links and marketing data; clinical records are retained under the legal retention obligation and put beyond further use. This has to be built in phase 0, not bolted on.
Connectivity in the resorts is unreliable.
Which is exactly why the clinician client is offline-first with an encrypted local store and reconciliation on reconnect. Any design that assumes a working connection at a Marsa Alam resort at 02:00 will fail in its first week.
What is the smallest version that proves this?
Phase 0 alone. Identity spine, no app, ninety days. If the re-identification rate across facilities is meaningfully above zero, the thesis is proven and everything downstream is justified. If it is near zero, we have learned that cheaply and we stop.

What I am asking for

One decision, three unblocks

A 90-day pilot · phase 0 plus a limited phase 1

10 resort clinics · 2 MedPark sites · target 3,000 registered guests

Success is measured on five numbers, agreed before we start
  • Registration rate — share of treated guests who create an HCIG ID
  • Re-identification rate — share of guests matched to a prior record across facilities
  • Claim rejection delta — app-documented encounters against paper, same period
  • Documentation time per encounter — clinician minutes, before and after
  • Guest NPS and the count of leaked calls we recaptured
Three decisions only IT can make
  • Data residency — EU-region primary store, or a documented alternative with counsel's sign-off
  • Brand architecture — one HCIG app across all facilities, or per-brand shells on one backend. I recommend one app; the identity layer is worthless if it is fragmented at the surface.
  • Integration access — a route to the resort check-in feed and to MedPark's HIS, even read-only, for the pilot
Budget outside existing headcount

Cloud and services for the pilot period, a DPIA with external counsel, and one penetration test before any real patient data is loaded. Everything else is time we already pay for.

If the room only remembers one line

HCIG treats hundreds of thousands of international patients and cannot say how many of them it has ever met twice. Fixing that is not a marketing project — it is the missing spine of a group that grew to 150 care points without one, and the guest app is simply the most profitable thing we can hang on it.
