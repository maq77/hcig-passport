01

The bet, in one page

What we are asking the board to back

HCIG treats hundreds of thousands of international patients and cannot say how many it has met twice. Every encounter is anonymous, one-time, and forgotten the moment the guest boards a plane. HCIG Passport turns each of those encounters into a permanent relationship — and each relationship into recurring revenue.

Why now

Healthcare is no longer a backup service — it is part of the guest experience. Hotels are being held responsible for guest safety, insurers are squeezing cost per case, and international standards are becoming a requirement rather than a differentiator. We already hold the accreditations and the estate. What we do not hold is the guest relationship. This is the asset the app creates.

The moat

Switching cost we create

A guest with three years of history on their HCIG ID will not restart that record at a competitor clinic for the sake of a cheaper consultation. Every encounter deepens the moat.

The multiplier

Families, not individuals

Families are the majority of resort guests. One install captures four or five patient identities. We should measure installs, but we should target identities per install.

The honest caveat

Year-one is a fraction

Ramp is real. Plan year one at roughly 35% of steady state — about €0.72M — and judge the pilot on rates, not absolute revenue.

04

How we reach the guest

Three moments, three completely different playbooks

Our guest is reachable in exactly three windows: when they are planning at home, when they have landed and something has gone wrong, and when they are back home deciding where to go next year. Most healthcare marketing only ever plays the second one.

Window 1 · pre-trip

Before they fly

Four to six weeks out, in their own country and language. The cheapest install we will ever buy, because they are calm and comparing.

  • Google Ads — Search in DE, RU, CS, IT, FR, PL. Travel-health intent
  • Yandex Direct for Russia. Google is not the search engine there
  • Meta & TikTok travel-intent and Egypt-interest audiences
  • Tour operator inserts in TUI / FTI / Anex booking confirmations
  • Insurer co-marketing — Allianz and AXA already fund presence in the assistance journey
  • Local networks per market — Seznam in Czechia, dive and travel forums
Window 2 · in destination

The moment it goes wrong

Highest intent in the entire funnel. They are in pain, in a foreign country, and searching on a phone. This is where Google Maps decides who they call.

  • Google Maps Ads — sponsored pins for "doctor near me", "clinic", "pharmacy" inside Hurghada, Marsa Alam, Sahl Hasheesh
  • Google Business Profile for every one of our facilities — hours, languages, photos, reviews. Free, and it beats any ad
  • Google Search — "Arzt Hurghada", "врач Хургада", "doctor Red Sea"
  • Geo-fenced Meta & TikTok around resort clusters
  • In-room QR, wristband, TV channel, front desk script — zero CAC and our biggest lever
Window 3 · post-trip

The channel the app creates

Once the record exists, every past guest is a consented, addressable audience. This channel does not exist today and costs nothing to run.

  • Day-5 follow-up call — clinical value, and it opens the next conversation
  • Off-season dental and aesthetic offers at the moment they book next year's holiday
  • Saved treatment quotes that travel to the next trip
  • Referral — €10 each way. Families and dive groups travel together
  • Arrival detection — they land, we already know, we say welcome back
The unfair advantage, said plainly

We already own the most valuable advertising channel in this market, and we are not using it. Around 150 clinics sit inside resorts full of exactly our customer. An in-room card costs cents and converts better than any keyword we can buy. Paid media should be the accelerant — never the engine.

05

Referral — and the line we do not cross

The cheapest growth loop in consumer business. In healthcare it is also the fastest route to a criminal problem. Both are true.

Our guests travel in groups — families, dive clubs, couples booking together. A referred guest is often already flying to Egypt. That makes referral a natural fit. But healthcare referral is regulated, and the version most people reach for first is illegal in our largest market.

Build this

Guest refers guest, rewarded in credit

€10 Care Pass credit each way. The referrer is paid only when the referred guest completes a first encounter or buys a pass. Redeemable against Care Pass, dental, wellness and aesthetics.

  • A commercial product, not a clinical service
  • Self-funding — it sells the thing it discounts
  • Captures an identity, which is the real prize
Do not build this

A bounty for whoever brings a patient

Per-patient commission to hotel staff, guides, drivers or clinicians is fee-splitting. It is not a grey area in the markets that matter to us.

  • Germany — §299a/b StGB made paying or taking money for patient referrals a criminal offence in 2016; MBO-Ä §31 prohibits it outright
  • USA — our Clearwater arm sits under the federal Anti-Kickback Statute
  • Egypt — the Medical Syndicate code prohibits commission for referral
RewardVerdictReasoning
Care Pass creditYesA commercial subscription. No clinical service is being given away, and nothing is billed to an insurer.
Discount on dental, wellness, aestheticsYesElective and self-pay. The guest is the payer, so the guest can be discounted.
Property-level revenue share with a hotelYesA contracted commercial agreement for services rendered, invoiced to the company — not cash per head to an individual.
Free urgent consultationNoA clinical service used as an inducement. It also pays people to seek care they do not need, which is the opposite of what we sell.
Percentage off insured treatmentNoThe insurer pays, not the guest, so there is nothing to discount. Waiving co-pays breaches most direct-billing contracts.
Cash per patient to staff or partnersNoFee-splitting. Criminal exposure in Germany and the USA, and precisely what an insurer audit looks for.
Mechanics

How it is controlled

  • Reward released only on a verified first encounter or paid pass
  • One reward per referred HCIG ID, ever
  • No self-referral — passport, device and payment-method checks
  • Capped per account per season
  • Never applied to urgent or insured care
Economics

Where it actually earns its place

At roughly €20 all-in per converted referral, this competes with an in-room QR card that costs close to nothing. So it is not a general discount.

Point it at the guests our own clinics cannot reach: friends who have not booked yet, guests staying at non-partner hotels, and the off-season.

The bigger loop

We already own a better one

Family linking. One install captures four or five patient identities, at zero cost and with zero regulatory exposure.

Target identities per install before spending a euro on referral. It is the cheaper number to move.

The recommendation, in one line

Yes to referral — as a Care Pass credit, scoped to elective services, capped and fraud-controlled. No to a per-patient bounty in any form. The first is a growth loop; the second is a criminal risk in the market that sends us the most guests. Worth having counsel confirm the specifics before launch, but the safe design costs us nothing to adopt now.

Spend follows intent. A guest typing "doctor" into Maps two kilometres from our clinic is worth many times a cold impression in Hamburg. Start narrow in Hurghada, prove cost per encounter, then widen to Marsa Alam and the North Coast.

Permitted in-app inventory

Direct-sold only

Featured placements in the wellness and dental marketplace. Resort spa, dive centre and diagnostic partners. Insurer brand presence on the coverage screen. Sold by hand as flat monthly placements — our audience is seasonal and high-value, which is worth more than programmatic.

Non-negotiable

No ad networks. No health targeting.

No AdMob or programmatic SDK — passing health-app signals to an ad network is a GDPR breach waiting to be found, and the revenue would be trivial. No targeting on medical conditions; Article 9 makes it effectively unlawful. No advertising on triage, active-case or record screens. Raising this limit ourselves is what makes us credible to insurers.

07

Organic: SEO, GEO and content

The compounding channel · costs almost nothing and raises the return on every paid euro

Fix this before buying a single ad

We run three separate domains — healthcareig.com, 247clinic.net and medparkhospitals.com — competing for the same terms and splitting our authority three ways. Consolidate into hub-and-spoke: healthcareig.com as the authority hub with country subfolders /de/ /ru/ /cs/ /it/, brand sites as spokes with canonical links back. This is a few weeks of work and it lifts everything downstream.

Technical SEO

Be findable

  • hreflang for seven languages, Arabic right-to-left included
  • LocalBusiness + MedicalClinic schema on every facility page — this is what feeds Google Maps
  • One page per facility, with real photos, languages on shift and capability
  • Core Web Vitals — guests search on hotel wifi
GEO

Be recommended by AI

  • Guests increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity "what do I do if I get ill in Hurghada"
  • Write clean, factual, citable answers — structured, sourced, unambiguous
  • Keep our accreditations, coverage and languages in machine-readable form
  • Nobody in this market is doing this yet. The window is now
Content

Own the long tail

  • A blog per language answering the questions guests actually type
  • Low competition, high intent, compounding forever
  • Reviewed by a clinician — accuracy is the brand
  • Every article routes to the nearest facility and the app

Business development

One hotel group signed is worth ten thousand app installs

B2C gets us volume. B2B gets us distribution. A single agreement with a hotel group or a tour operator puts HCIG Passport in front of every guest they carry, at zero acquisition cost, for the life of the contract.

Target 1

Hotels & resort groups

The pitch: guest safety you can put in your brochure, a front desk that stops firefighting during a medical incident, and a revenue share on every Care Pass sold in your rooms.

  • Start with the ten properties where our relationship is already strongest
  • Unique QR per property so their contribution is provable
  • Let the results recruit the rest — hoteliers talk to each other
Target 2

Tour operators

The pitch: differentiate the Egypt product with accredited on-site healthcare, reduce the incidents that generate complaints and refunds.

  • TUI, FTI, DER Touristik, Anex, Coral — Germany first, then Czechia and Poland
  • Fund it with a Care Pass revenue share, not cash
  • Entry point is the product manager for Egypt, not head office
Target 3

Insurers & assistance

The pitch: lower cost per case, faster guarantee of payment, cleaner claims, and reporting they currently cannot get from anyone in this region.

  • Allianz, AXA, ERGO, UNIQA, ERV, ADAC, HanseMerkur, Mondial
  • Ask them to co-fund the app that reduces their claim cost
  • Our denial-rate data is the whole argument — bring numbers, not slides
How we get in the room

Four routes, in order of yield

  • Trade events — ITB Berlin, WTM London, Arabian Travel Market. Where these deals actually get done
  • LinkedIn — targeted outreach to Egypt product managers and insurer network heads, plus visible thought leadership from our own board
  • Direct email — short, specific, one number in the subject line. Not a brochure
  • healthcareig.com — a real partner page with case studies and the accreditations above the fold
Our strongest opening line

We are the founder of UCA MENA and the official GHA partner in Egypt. That sentence opens doors that a price list never will. Accreditation is not compliance overhead — it is our most effective business development asset, and it should lead every conversation.

10

What the board should watch

Six numbers · everything else is decoration

01 · proves the thesis

Re-identification rate

Of guests treated this season, how many were matched to a record from a previous visit. If this rises, we have built an asset. If it does not, we have built an app.

02 · proves the economics

Claim denial rate

App-documented encounters against paper, same period. The single strongest figure in the whole case, and it is measurable within one quarter.

03 · proves the reach

Cost per registered guest

By channel. Expect near zero from in-room QR and single-digit euros from Google Maps. If a channel cannot beat the front desk, cut it.

04 · proves the value

Lifetime value per guest

Across every facility and every visit. A number HCIG has never been able to calculate, and the one that sets what we can afford to spend.

05 · proves the capture

Identities per install

Families are the majority of our guests. Target four, not one.

06 · proves the quality

Leaked calls recaptured

Hotel calls that today end without an HCIG encounter. Nobody tracks this. The moment we do, the leakage line stops being an estimate.

If the board remembers one line

We are not building an app. We are building the guest relationship we have never owned — on top of an estate, a workforce and a set of accreditations we already have. The app is simply the most profitable thing we can hang on it.
