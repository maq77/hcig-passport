Three data problems to fix before launch. Premier Le Rêve’s coordinates are copied from another resort and would drop the map pin in the wrong place. Every clinic shares one placeholder WhatsApp number. And the Steigenberger record’s name may not match the hotel in the brief.

A better idea, since you asked for one

Do not hand-build three pages and then copy them 27 more times by hand. Build one data-driven template fed by the clinic record plus a small content table. Launch the three now. After that, any of your 30 clinics becomes a new landing page by filling in content, not by writing code. That turns a one-off task into a local-search asset for the whole coast, and every future page inherits the same correct schema, canonical and tracking, so quality never drifts.

Deliverable 1

Recommended URL structure

Your instinct to lead with location is right for SEO. Refined:

Steigenberger, Ras Soma/soma-bay/steigenberger-clinic
Amwaj Beach Club, Abu Soma/abu-soma/amwaj-beach-club-clinic
Premier Le Rêve, Sahl Hasheesh/sahl-hasheesh/premier-le-reve-clinic

Location first builds an area folder for each destination, so a future /sahl-hasheesh/ hub page can later win the pure area searches (“clinic sahl hasheesh”) without competing with the hotel pages. It also scales cleanly: 30 clinics fall into six or seven area folders that read as one topical cluster on one authoritative domain, which is exactly the “keep the authority on the main site” goal in the brief.

  • Ras Soma or Soma Bay. “Soma Bay” is the recognised search term, so the slug uses it, while the visible title keeps “Steigenberger Ras Soma” for exact-match relevance.
  • Le Rêve accents. Most guests type without the circumflex, so the slug is le-reve and the visible copy keeps the correct “Le Rêve”.
Deliverable 2

These are hyper-local, high-intent, near-zero-volume searches. Keyword tools will report almost no monthly volume for “doctor Steigenberger Ras Soma”, and that is expected. A guest typing it is in the hotel, unwell, ready to call. The strategy is not chasing volume. It is being the single most exactly relevant result for every phrasing, in every guest language, then mining Search Console for the real queries once the pages are live. For terms this specific, that is where the true keyword data comes from.

Deliverable 3

Page structure, mobile first

One template, conversion first, no corporate preamble. The guest sees how to reach a doctor before anything else. Order on a phone, top to bottom:

  1. Sticky top bar. Logo, and a Call button always visible.
  2. Hero, above the fold. H1 “Need a Doctor at [Hotel]?”, one reassuring line, then three thumb-sized buttons: Call Now, WhatsApp (green), Find the Clinic. A trust strip below: inside the resort, response in minutes, insurance assistance.
  3. Where to find us. Plain-language location inside the hotel, the map, a Directions button.
  4. What we treat. Your 11 real services, phrased for a worried guest: consultation, examination, minor illness and injury, medication, laboratory coordination, ambulance, hospital referral, insurance help, medical reports, fit to fly and diving certificates.
  5. For international guests. Languages spoken, insurance help, medical reports for claims.
  6. Photographs. Real clinic photos with descriptive alt text.
  7. Hours and contact. 24/7 stated plainly, phone, WhatsApp, address.
  8. FAQ. Six to eight questions from real guest searches.
  9. Sticky bottom bar. Call and WhatsApp within reach the whole way down. This one element usually moves conversion the most.

Deliverable 5

Technical SEO

  • Unique everything. Title, description, H1, body, FAQ and alt text written per hotel. No cloned pages with a swapped name.
  • Canonical self-referencing, absolute, lower case, on every page.
  • Schema. Yes to the brief’s question: MedicalClinic and LocalBusiness both apply per page, with address, geo, phone, WhatsApp, 24/7 hours, area served and services, plus FAQPage and BreadcrumbList. The geo and address come from data you already own, once Premier Le Rêve’s coordinates are fixed.
  • hreflang across any German or Polish versions, with x-default.
  • Internal links from the main site to each page and across siblings, so none is an orphan.
  • Sitemap and indexing. Added to the sitemap automatically, submitted in Search Console, indexing requested at launch, rendered head verified.

Four site-wide faults to fix first, because these pages depend on them

The sitemap currently returns an error and lists nothing. The site has no working “page not found”, so every missing URL returns a valid page to Google. Four existing pages render a blank title from missing database rows, the same mechanism these new pages rely on. And index and canonical behaviour must be checked on the live rendered page, not the template. All four are documented and fixable.

Deliverable 6

Tracking

Assumes GA4, Search Console and the group tracking tag are in place. Note: the live site currently shows no GA4, so the property may still need creating. Confirm the measurement ID.

  • GA4 key events, each marked as a conversion: call_click, whatsapp_click, directions_click, contact_action. Page views are automatic.
  • Every event carries the hotel and area as parameters, so one report compares all clinics side by side.
  • Per-page reporting. Each landing page is a distinct URL with distinct parameters, so GA4 and Search Console segment them cleanly. Search Console shows impressions, clicks and the real queries per page, which is also the keyword feedback loop.
  • Group tracking tag added to the template, with an edge check, since Cloudflare in front can cache or block it.
Deliverable 7

What we need from you

The template is blocked on these. Per hotel:

  1. Steigenberger identity. Is the database’s “Steigenberger soma bay” the same property as “Steigenberger Resort Ras Soma”? If two different Steigenbergers, which one?
  2. Inside or serving? Is the clinic physically inside the hotel, or serving it from nearby? The H1 depends on this being exact. It is a medical claim.
  3. Location inside each hotel, in plain words (building, floor, near which landmark).
  4. Real WhatsApp number per clinic. The stored value is a placeholder.
  5. Real phone number per clinic, and whether one central number serves all.
  6. Opening hours. Confirm genuinely 24/7, or the real hours.
  7. Verified GPS coordinates, especially Premier Le Rêve.
  8. Clinic photographs per hotel: signage or exterior, interior, staff or doctor, equipment. Real, not stock.
  9. Doctor names and credentials, if these are to be shown. Touches compliance.
  10. Languages per page. German is a strong candidate for all three.
  11. Insurance partners accepted at each, if named.
  12. Affiliation and trademark. Are these clinics officially inside or endorsed by each hotel, or independent clinics serving the hotel’s guests? Using a hotel name in a URL and title is fine when it is factually accurate and implies no endorsement that does not exist. If there is an official arrangement we can say so, and it strengthens the page. If not, we phrase it as “serving guests of”. This must be right before launch, legally and for trust.

Platform, once: confirm or create the 247clinic GA4 property and share the measurement ID; confirm Search Console access for 247clinic.net.

The order of work

Sequence

  1. You approve the URL structure, the approach and this plan.
  2. Fix the four site-wide SEO faults, since the pages depend on them. Local first.
  3. Live keyword and query validation. Tune titles and H1s.
  4. Build the data-driven landing template locally, on the recovered source.
  5. Write each page’s unique content. Copy goes to review.
  6. Fix the three data problems: coordinates, WhatsApp numbers, Steigenberger identity.
  7. Wire schema and tracking, validate locally.
  8. You review on a private preview and approve.
  9. Publish, submit to Search Console, request indexing.
  10. Watch Search Console for real queries, and iterate.

Development on the final pages does not start until steps 1 to 3 are approved.
