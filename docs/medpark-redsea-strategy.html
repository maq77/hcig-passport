01Website architecture

Own the coast road, not the lobby

The Red Sea coast is not a metro area. It is a single road running roughly 270 km south from Hurghada, with resort clusters strung along it at known distances and almost nothing in between. That linear geography is the most important fact about your SEO, because it means every location query is really a distance question: if this happens to me here, how far am I from a hospital?

Your two branches sit at fixed points on that line. Everything else is coverage.

Spelling, settled

Use El Quseir. It appears 142 times across your site, in your schema and on your Google listing. You write “Al Qusier” in messages; that transposition appears nowhere in your estate and should not start now. One spelling, everywhere, or you split your own signal.

1.1  The location silhouette page, and why hotel pages cannot do this job

A hotel-oriented page answers “we serve guests of this resort”. It ties your authority to somebody else's brand, competes with the hotel's own pages, and produces exactly the long beach resort ranking sitting in your data today.

A location silhouette page answers a different question: “what happens to anyone in this place who needs a hospital”. It is written for the place, not for a customer of the place, which is why it can rank for the place's name and be useful to residents, expats, workers and tourists at the same time.

You currently have zero of these. Your 50 URLs cover services and two branch pages. There is no page that is about Sahl Hasheesh, Makadi Bay, Safaga, Soma Bay or Marsa Alam.

Do not skip this

Every one of these needs a German and Polish twin at /de/ and /pl/, registered in hreflang.php and picked up by the dynamic sitemap. A location page in English only wastes the audience that is already searching: krankenhaus hurghada and hurghada szpital are in your data today.

Trade guest-of-the-hotel traffic for city-wide emergency traffic

The shift is not a word swap. It is a change in who the page is addressed to. Hotel-framed terms assume the reader is a guest and you are an amenity. City-framed and expat-framed terms assume the reader is a person in a place and you are the hospital that serves it.

2.2  How the internal linking has to work

Pillars and location pages are not two separate content programmes. They are one structure, and the direction of the links is what tells Google which page is the authority for which query.

  • Pillars link down to location pages with the location's own name as the anchor. “If you are in Makadi Bay, this is your nearest emergency department” linking on hospital in Makadi Bay. Never “click here”, never “read more”.
  • Location pages link up to one pillar only, the one matching their language and intent. One clean parent, not four.
  • Every page links to the emergency contact in the body text, not just in the header. A header link repeated site-wide carries far less weight than a contextual one.
  • Location pages link sideways to their two nearest neighbours and nothing else. That reproduces the coast road in your link graph, which is exactly the relationship you want understood.
  • Both branch pages link to each other with the other's city as anchor, so the two-site network is legible rather than looking like two unrelated businesses.

Register every new article in news/related.php. It is already the single article register feeding the index, the related block and the sitemap, so one entry there does all three jobs.

3.1  Cleaning the profile without losing reviews

Your listings carry 4.8 from 271 reviews at the Health Hub and 4.7 from 187 at El Quseir. That is the most valuable asset in this entire plan and it is not replaceable. Every instruction below is written to protect it.

Never do this

Do not create a new listing for a location that already has one, for any reason, including “the old one is wrong” or “we cannot get access”. Duplicates are the most common cause of suspension in healthcare, they split reviews permanently, and merging them back is not guaranteed. Fix the listing you have.

  1. Set the primary category to Hospital. This is the single biggest lever you have for “hospital in hurghada”, larger than anything on the website. You confirmed both are licensed full hospitals, so this is accurate. Your site schema was corrected to Hospital; the listing has to agree.
  2. Set secondary categories that describe departments, not amenities. Emergency room, medical diagnostic imaging centre, intensive care unit, dentist. Avoid anything that reads as hospitality.
  3. Fix the name to match the signage exactly. “MedPark Health Hub Hurghada” and “MedPark Hospital El Quseir”. Do not append keywords such as “Hospital in Hurghada” to the name field. Keyword stuffing the name is the second most common suspension trigger and it is easy for a competitor to report.
  4. Turn on every attribute that says hospital. Open 24 hours, emergency services, wheelchair-accessible entrance, on-site parking, gender-neutral toilets, languages spoken. Attributes are a direct category signal, not decoration.
  5. Set hours to 24 hours, seven days, and add the emergency department as a separate service if the profile offers it. Never leave holiday hours blank.
  6. Point each listing at its matching page, Health Hub to /healthhub.php, El Quseir to /medparkhospital.php. Both pointing at the home page wastes the strongest link either listing has.
  7. Rewrite the description around the region and the capability. Draft below.
  8. Photographs: exterior first. A street-visible emergency entrance with signage is the most category-defining image you can upload, because it is what a reviewer checks when a listing is reported. Then ambulance, reception, CT room, ward. Geotag nothing; Google ignores it and it looks manipulative.
  9. Change one thing, then wait. A burst of edits on a healthcare listing can trigger a manual review. Category first, alone. Then wait a week. Then attributes and hours. Then description and photos.

3.2  Getting reviews that mention the place

Where I have to push back on the brief

You asked for a script that prompts patients to leave reviews containing specific keywords. I will not write that one, because dictating review content breaches Google's review policies and is a documented suspension and review-removal trigger. It also reads as fake to anyone scanning your reviews, which costs you the trust the 4.8 rating buys.

The framework below gets the same outcome legitimately. You cannot script the words, but you can control the question you ask, and the question determines what people naturally write about. Ask about the place and the moment, and the place and the moment appear in the review, in their own words, which ranks better anyway.

The ask, and when to make it

  • Timing beats wording. Ask at discharge, in person, while relief is the strongest emotion, and follow with one message the same day. Not three days later.
  • One channel, one link. A short link straight to the review form for the correct branch. Two branches means two links; sending an El Quseir patient to the Hurghada listing corrupts both.
  • Ask everyone. Do not screen for happy patients first. Gating reviews by predicted sentiment is against policy and is detectable.
  • Never offer anything in return. No discount, no priority, no gift.

3.3  Suspensions, before they happen

Healthcare listings are suspended for a predictable set of reasons. Check all of these before you appeal anything, because a failed appeal is harder to overturn the second time.

  • Name on the listing does not match signage and website
  • Keywords appended to the business name
  • Address is not a staffed, signposted, street-accessible location
  • Category does not match what the business demonstrably is
  • A burst of edits in a short window
  • A duplicate listing for the same location

Photograph the exterior signage and the emergency entrance now, before you need them. An appeal is won with evidence that the place exists and is what it says it is.

04AI engine optimisation

How to be the name an AI says out loud

When somebody asks ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini “is there a proper hospital in Hurghada”, the answer is assembled from two different things, and it is worth being precise about which is which, because they need different work.

What the model already believes

Associations formed during training, from how often and in what company your name appeared across the web. This is slow, it cannot be edited, and it is built almost entirely from third-party sources. If forums, directories and travel writing describe you as a hotel clinic, that is the association, no matter what your own site says.

What it reads at question time

Live retrieval from search and direct fetches. This is fast, it responds to your site within days, and it is where structured data and clear page text matter enormously, because a retrieval system extracts claims rather than reading prose.

So the strategy is two-track. Fix what is machine-readable on your own domain now, which is section 1 and pays off within weeks. Then change what the wider web says about you, which is slow, compounding, and the only thing that shifts the trained association.

4.1  Make your own pages extractable

  • State facts in complete sentences that survive being quoted alone. “MedPark Health Hub is a 24-hour hospital on Sahl Hasheesh Road in Hurghada with an emergency department, intensive care and CT imaging on site” is retrievable. “We are here for you around the clock” is not.
  • Put the answer directly under the heading that asks the question. Retrieval takes the passage under the matching heading. Do not bury it three paragraphs in.
  • Give every distinguishing fact a number. Ambulances, beds, ICU capacity, languages, years open, insurers billed directly. Numbers are what a model repeats.
  • Use FAQPage schema on the location pages, with the questions phrased the way people actually ask them.
  • Fix the two FAQ answers that are now wrong. “Is MedPark a hospital or a clinic?” and “What's the difference between MedPark Hospital and MedPark Health Hubs?” both still answer from the old position that one site was a clinic. Those two answers are precisely what a model quotes when asked the category question, and they currently argue against you.
  • Do not block AI crawlers in robots.txt. Confirm GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended are permitted. Being invisible to them is a decision, and it should be a deliberate one.

4.2  Change what the rest of the web says

Ranked by how much a crawler weighs the source against how hard it is to earn. Nothing here involves buying links.

The common thread is that every one of these produces a sentence written by somebody else that contains your name next to the word hospital and a place name. That sentence is the unit of currency in AI visibility. Fifty of them across trusted sources will move the association further than any amount of work on your own copy.

You can already measure this

Your dashboard has an AI visibility checker in lib/aicheck.php, and it has already recorded a position-1 result once. Set a fixed question set now, before the work starts, so you have a baseline: “hospital in Hurghada”, “Krankenhaus Hurghada”, “szpital Hurghada”, “emergency room Sahl Hasheesh”, “hospital near Marsa Alam”. Re-run monthly and watch whether you are named, and whether you are described as a hospital or as a clinic.
